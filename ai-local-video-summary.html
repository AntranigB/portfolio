Vibe-coded project: I supplied the idea, requirements, and feedback; AI wrote the implementation.

I wanted to summarize recordings without sending their content to the cloud. It was also a way to test how useful local AI actually is and get more familiar with it.

What it does

The macOS app imports recordings, transcribes them locally, and generates notes linked back to timestamps. Speaker diarization separates voices into estimated speaker clusters; review tools let me listen, name speakers, and correct assignments. Optional screen analysis adds visual evidence. Diarization is imperfect: speaker labels need review, and a model-generated summary is not a guarantee of accuracy.

How it was built

Vibe-coded project: I supplied the idea, requirements, and feedback; AI wrote the implementation.

Technology: SwiftUI and AVKit; Python, MLX Whisper, sherpa-onnx, FFmpeg, SQLite, LM Studio / Qwen.

What I learned

This also became a lesson in user design: thinking about how someone chooses settings, sees progress, reviews a transcript, and corrects a speaker label. Those interactions matter as much as getting a summary to appear.

Current status

Ongoing. The queue, transcript review, speaker corrections, and local model workflow are implemented; accuracy and usability remain areas to improve.
